How the image becomes music

  • The landscape picks the piece. Ocean, coast, desert, rainforest, green lands, ice, city and night each have their own tempo, rhythm, accompaniment and live soundscape (surf and gulls, desert wind, insects and frogs, birds and a stream, cracking ice, traffic and crowds).
  • The location picks the local sound. The same landscape sounds different in different parts of the world: Egypt (double harmonic scale, qanun, ney, frame drum, strings in unison), Anatolia (makam Hicaz, bağlama, ney, darbuka, Bosphorus ferry horns in Istanbul), Aegean (Kürdi mode, bouzouki tremolo, kaval, guitar, def), Japan (in scale, koto, shakuhachi, taiko, temple bells, Tokyo crossing signals), China and Southeast Asia (pentatonic, guzheng, erhu, dizi, gongs and woodblocks), India (raag Khamaj, sitar, bansuri, tanpura, tabla), Africa (kalimba, djembe), Australia (didgeridoo, clapsticks), the Andes (quena, charango, bombo), Brazil (bossa nova guitar, samba percussion), the Celtic isles (tin whistle, fiddle, harp, bodhrán, pipe drones), Mexico (mariachi trumpets, violins, vihuela) and the Central Asian steppe (dombra, galloping rhythm, overtone singing).
  • Brightness → melody. Brighter columns (clouds, ice, desert) climb higher. The melody leans on the notes of the current chord, like a real tune.
  • Surface → instrument. Water is played by a flute, vegetation by violins, bare land by brighter violins, cloud and ice by a celesta.
  • Busy landscapes → bigger arrangement. Calm stretches stay quiet with strings only. Colorful, varied stretches bring in harp, basses, timpani and a second violin line.
  • Coastlines and cloud edges → harp strum. You hear the border when the surface changes.
  • NASA data layers change the music. Sea or land surface temperature makes warm places faster with a bright shimmer and cold places slower with a low breath; active fires crackle under the playhead and bring a dark brass swell; rain adds a rain soundscape, slows the music and can bring thunder; denser vegetation (NDVI) brings more birdsong.
  • Move the map while it plays and the music follows the new view.
  • Explore by touch lets you drag a finger (or use the arrow keys) over the view and hear the spot underneath: each surface has its own instrument and brighter places sound higher. It is made so that people who cannot see the image can still explore Earth by ear.
  • Save 30 s clip renders the current view offline at full quality and gives you a WAV file, smooth even on a slow phone.
